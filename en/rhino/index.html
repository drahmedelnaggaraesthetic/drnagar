The natural light lines

Nasal Bridge (Nasal Dorsum)

The nasal bridge is made of the nasal bones in the upper third and the roof cartilages in the lower two-thirds; it is responsible for the straightness of the nose and the soft reflection of light along its sides.

How we protect it: With the preservation piezo technique (Push-Down / Let-Down), we lower the bony-cartilaginous hump from below while the bridge roof stays intact as one piece, which prevents irregularities and keeps the appearance completely natural.
Fact 1: airy silicone splints instead of the painful traditional packing
The era of long cotton packing that stuck to the nasal lining and caused severe pain on removal is over. Today we use soft, hollow medical silicone splints (Doyle Airway Splints) with an internal airway that allows partial breathing after surgery. They do not adhere to tissue, so they are removed in the clinic within seconds, calmly and with virtually no pain.
Fact 2: the chin-balancing trick (profile balancing)
In the profile view, the eye always judges nose size relative to chin projection along the Ricketts E-Line. A chin set back by only a few millimeters makes a normal nose look longer and more prominent than it really is; a subtle nasal refinement combined with modest chin balancing gives far finer harmony than over-reducing the nose.
Fact 3: the engineering of the male versus the female nose
Successful rhinoplasty respects each person's anatomical identity. In the male nose we preserve a strong, straight bridge and a nasolabial angle of 90° to 95°, with no tip curvature or excessive tip rotation. In the female nose we favor a smooth bridge that is straight or gently curved, with an angle of 95° to 105° that gives natural refinement and balance with the facial features.

What do you see in the mirror now? (Week 1 · Medium skin)
When the splint comes off, the nose looks swollen and slightly over-rotated; this initial lift is normal and will gradually come down.
Advice for this stage: Do not judge the final shape on splint-removal day; watery swelling hides the finest details for now.
